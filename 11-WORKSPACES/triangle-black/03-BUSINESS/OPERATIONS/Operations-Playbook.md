## 1. Engineering Supply Operations

### 1.1 Supplier Qualification
- All suppliers must complete a supplier registration form
- Submit: company profile, trade license, bank details, product catalog, quality certifications
- Evaluation criteria: pricing competitiveness (30%), delivery reliability (25%), quality (25%), payment terms (10%), responsiveness (10%)
- Approved suppliers added to supplier directory
- Annual re-qualification required

### 1.2 Procurement Process
1. Requisition created by project manager or engineer
2. Requisition approved by budget holder
3. RFQ sent to minimum 3 qualified suppliers
4. Quotes evaluated on total cost (price + delivery + quality)
5. Purchase order issued to selected supplier
6. PO approved per authorization matrix
7. Supplier confirms PO and provides delivery date
8. Goods received and inspected at site
9. Three-way match: PO = delivery note = invoice
10. Invoice submitted for payment

### 1.3 Urgent Procurement
- Emergency purchases under $500: verbal approval from ops manager, PO issued within 24 hours
- Emergency purchases over $500: written approval from director

### 1.4 Inventory Management
- Minimum stock levels defined for critical spares
- Monthly stock count for A-items, quarterly for B-items, annual for C-items
- Inventory accuracy target: 95%+

## 2. Engineering Contracting Operations

### 2.1 Project Setup
1. Contract signed and filed
2. Project created in operations portal
3. Project team assigned (PM, engineers, supervisors)
4. Kickoff meeting with client
5. Site access, safety induction, and permits arranged
6. Baseline schedule submitted

### 2.2 Execution
- Daily progress reports submitted by site supervisor
- Weekly progress review with PM
- Monthly client progress meeting
- Change requests documented with cost and schedule impact
- Approved changes added as contract amendments

### 2.3 Quality Control
- Inspection hold points defined in project plan
- Third-party testing where specified
- Non-conformance reports for any deviation
- Corrective action tracked to closure

### 2.4 Safety
- Site-specific safety plan required for every project
- Daily toolbox talks before work starts
- PPE compliance mandatory
- Incident reporting within 2 hours
- Monthly safety statistics reported

## 3. Engineering Operations

### 3.1 Work Order Lifecycle
1. Work request received (from client, inspection, or preventive schedule)
2. Work order created with priority classification
3. Work order assigned to available engineer
4. Engineer acknowledges assignment
5. Engineer completes work with checklist
6. Quality verification (random sampling: 10%)
7. Client acknowledgment
8. Work order closed

### 3.2 Priority Classification
| Priority | Response Time | Resolution Time |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | 1 hour | 4 hours |
| High | 4 hours | 24 hours |
| Medium | 24 hours | 72 hours |
| Low | 72 hours | 7 days |

### 3.3 Preventive Maintenance
- PM schedules generated monthly
- PM completion target: 90%+ on time
- Overdue PMs escalated weekly
- PM checklists standardized per asset type
- Historical PM data used to adjust frequency

## 4. Maintenance Operations

### 4.1 Asset Registration
- Every registered asset requires: make, model, serial number, installation date, warranty period, location, category
- Barcode or QR code label affixed to each asset
- Asset register updated on installation, transfer, or disposal

### 4.2 Maintenance Types
| Type | Description | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Preventive | Scheduled per manufacturer spec | Monthly/Quarterly/Annually |
| Corrective | Fix broken equipment | On-demand |
| Predictive | Data-driven condition monitoring | Continuous |
| Condition-based | Inspect and decide | Per threshold |

### 4.3 Spare Parts Management
- Critical spares identified per asset category
- Minimum stock levels maintained
- Consumed spares recorded against work orders
- Stock count variance < 3% quarterly target
