# Laundry Operations

## Overview

Hotel laundry is a high-volume industrial operation that processes bed sheets, towels, bathrobes, table linen, uniforms, and guest laundry. A typical full-service hotel generates 10-15 kg of linen per occupied room per day. Laundry accounts for 2-4% of hotel energy costs and is a major consumer of water and steam.

## Laundry Volume by Hotel Size

| Hotel Size | Rooms | Linen (kg/day)* | Staff | Equipment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small | < 80 | 200-600 | 2-3 | Washer-extractors + dryers |
| Mid-scale | 80-200 | 600-2000 | 4-8 | Tunnels or batch washers |
| Upscale | 200-400 | 2000-5000 | 8-15 | Tunnel washer + ironers |
| Luxury/Resort | 400+ | 5000-12000+ | 15-30 | Full industrial laundry |
| *10-15 kg/occupied room/day including all linen categories |

## Laundry Equipment

### Washer-Extractors

| Size (kg) | Application | Wash Cycle | Extraction G-Force |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15-25 | Guest laundry, small loads | 45-90 min | 200-400 G |
| 25-50 | Stand-alone linen processing | 45-90 min | 200-400 G |
| 50-100 | Medium hotel primary washer | 45-90 min | 300-400 G |
| 100-200 | Large hotel, high volume | 45-90 min | 300-400 G |

**Key brands:** Miele, Electrolux, Primus, Girbau, UniMac, Kannegiesser

**Key components:**
- Drum (stainless steel) with lift ribs
- Motor with VFD for variable speed
- Water valves (hot, cold, steam)
- Chemical injection ports
- Microprocessor control (programmable cycles)
- Door interlock (safety — cannot open while drum moving)

### Tunnel / Batch Washer

For hotels > 200 rooms. Continuous batch processing.

**How it works:**
1. Linen loaded into compartments (pockets)
2. Moves through tunnel in counter-flow (linen forward, water backward)
3. Zones: Pre-wash → Main wash → Bleach → Rinse → Rinse → Sour → Extract
4. At end: High-G centrifugal extraction (50-60% moisture retention)
5. Capacity: 30-60 kg per pocket, 8-20 pockets → 500-2000+ kg/hour

**Advantages over washer-extractors:**
- Much lower water and energy consumption per kg
- Consistent quality (automated, repeatable)
- Higher throughput
- Lower labor cost per kg

### Dryers

| Type | Capacity | Heat Source | Drying Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tumble dryer | 30-100 kg | Steam, gas, electric | 30-60 min |
| Tumble dryer (gas) | 30-100 kg | LPG / Natural gas | 20-40 min |

**Moisture retention after drying:** 3-5% (for ironing), 0% (full dry — for storage)

### Flatwork Ironers (Roller Ironers)

Used for sheets, pillow cases, tablecloths, and napkins.

| Type | Rollers | Speed | Capacity (sheets/hr) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single-roll | 1 | 5-15 m/min | 50-100 |
| Multi-roll (2-4) | 2-4 | 15-40 m/min | 200-400 |
| Multi-roll (4-6) | 4-6 | 30-60 m/min | 400-800 |

**Components:**
- Heated rollers (steam or thermal fluid)
- Feeding conveyor (for operators to feed linen)
- Folding section (automatic cross-fold + longitudinal fold)
- Stacking conveyor
- Padding (roller covering material — replaced annually)

### Finishing Equipment

| Equipment | Function | Used For |
|---|---|---|
| Press (tunnel finisher) | Remove wrinkles | Uniforms, table linen |
| Steam cabinet | De-wrinkle delicate items | Guest laundry, uniforms |
| Spotting board | Pre-treatment of stains | All items before washing |
| Tumbler (conditioning) | Fluff-dry, partial dry | Towels, terry items |

## Laundry Flow

### In-House (On-Site) Laundry Flow

```
Soiled Linen Collection (from housekeeping chutes/carts)
        ↓
Sorting (by type, color, soil level)
        ↓
Pre-treatment (spotting, stain treatment)
        ↓
Washing (washer-extractors or tunnel washer)
        ↓
Extraction (centrifuge to remove water)
        ↓
Drying (towels, terry) ←→ Ironing (sheets, table linen)
        ↓
Folding (manual or automatic)
        ↓
Storage / Dispatch on carts to floors
```

### Flow for Sheets and Pillowcases (Automated Line)

Tunnel washer → Centrifuge → Shake-out conveyor → Feed conveyor → Roller ironer → Cross-fold → Longitudinal fold → Stack → Count → Tie → Dispatch

### Flow for Towels (Terry)

Washer → Centrifuge → Tumble dryer (fluff dry) → Folding table (manual or folder) → Stack → Dispatch

## Laundry Chemicals

| Chemical | Function | Typical Dose (per kg linen) |
|---|---|---|
| Detergent (alkaline) | Main cleaning agent | 4-8 g/kg |
| Bleach (chlorine or oxygen) | Whitening, stain removal | 2-5 g/kg |
| Sour (acid rinse) | Neutralize alkalinity, brighten | 1-3 g/kg |
| Softener | Reduce fabric friction | 1-3 g/kg |
| Starch | Stiffness for table linen | 1-5 g/kg |
| Stain remover (enzyme) | Protein stain digestion | As needed spot treatment |

### Water Temperature by Fabric

| Fabric | Wash Temp | Rinse Temp | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| White cotton (sheets, towels) | 70-85°C | Cold | High temp for hygiene |
| Colored cotton | 40-60°C | Cold | Prevent bleeding |
| Polyester blends | 60°C | Cold | Lower temp, faster |
| Wool / Delicate | 30-40°C | Cold | Gentle cycle |
| Table linen | 70-80°C | Cold | Stain removal + hygiene |

## Outsource vs. In-House

| Factor | In-House | Outsource (Commercial) |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per kg | $0.30-0.60 | $0.50-1.00 |
| Control | Full control | Dependent on vendor |
| Quality | Consistent (if well-managed) | Variable |

**Sharm El Sheikh context:** Most resorts have in-house laundries because of remote location, high volume, and cost control. Some smaller hotels use outsourced services in nearby Sharm industrial area.

## Quality Standards

### Guest Room Linen Quality Metrics
- **Whiteness index:** > 150 (measured on spectrophotometer)
- **Tensile strength:** Retain > 80% of original after 50 washes
- **No stains:** 100% inspection after processing
- **No holes / tears:** Reject at sorting stage
- **Folding standard:** Consistent size and shape
- **Packaging:** Wrapped in paper bands, sealed in plastic for VIP

### HACCP for Laundry (Food Safety)
- Sorting area separate from clean area
- Positive air pressure in clean linen storage
- Clean linen stored on shelves (not floor)
- Transport covered and enclosed
- Staff hand hygiene

## Common Laundry Problems

| Problem | Cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Graying / Yellowing | Insufficient detergent, low temperature, iron in water | Adjust wash formula, check water quality |
| Stains not removing | Wrong wash cycle, old stains set by heat | Pre-treatment, correct wash program |
| Linen tearing | Overloading, sharp edges on equipment | Load per spec, check drum condition |
| High water consumption | Wrong program, leaking valves | Program optimization, valve maintenance |
| High chemical cost | Over-dosing, wrong chemical selection | Auto-dosing + regular consumption review |
| Low productivity | Poor workflow layout, equipment breakdown | Layout redesign, preventive maintenance |
| Musty smell | Incomplete drying, microbial growth | Ensure complete drying, check ventilation |
| Linen loss / theft | No tracking system | RFID linen tracking |
| Excessive wear | Over-bleaching, excessive mechanical action | Optimize chemical formula and cycle time |

## AI Opportunities

- **Predictive Maintenance on Equipment:** Vibration + current monitoring on washer motors, dryer burners, ironer rollers → predict failures
- **Wash Formula Optimization:** ML matches wash program to soil level and fabric type, optimizing chemical and energy use
- **Linen Quality Inspection:** Computer vision at folding station detects stains, holes, and wear → auto-sorts to reject or repair
- **Linen Lifecycle Prediction:** Track wash cycles (RFID tag) → predict remaining useful life per piece
- **Laundry Production Planning:** Forecast daily demand by occupancy and events → optimize staffing and equipment scheduling
- **Energy Optimization:** Model dryer and ironer energy consumption → optimize temperature, speed, and batch size
- **Chemical Dosing Control:** Real-time feedback from turbidity sensors → auto-adjust chemical dose for load
- **Stain Recognition:** Image of stain → AI identifies type and recommends pre-treatment protocol
