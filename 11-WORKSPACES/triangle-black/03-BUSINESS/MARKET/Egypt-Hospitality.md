# Egypt Hospitality Industry Overview

## Market Size & Growth

The Egypt hospitality market is valued at approximately **USD 21.54 billion in 2026**, with a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of **7.12%** projected through 2031, reaching an estimated **USD 30.39 billion**. This growth is driven by government tourism targets, infrastructure investment, and increasing international investor interest in Egyptian hospitality assets.

## Key Market Drivers

1. **Government Tourism Strategy** — Egypt's target of 30 million tourists by 2030 drives hotel development across all segments. The Ministry of Tourism has launched multiple investment incentive programs including tax holidays, land grants, and simplified licensing.

2. **Infrastructure Investment** — New airports (Sphinx International, New Administrative Capital), expanded metro systems, upgraded highways, and new coastal resorts are opening previously inaccessible destinations.

3. **International Brand Expansion** — Major global chains (Accor, Marriott, Hilton, IHG, Rotana) are actively expanding their Egyptian portfolios, with signed management agreements for 40+ new properties in the pipeline.

4. **Domestic Tourism Growth** — A rising middle class (projected 45% of population by 2030) and improved domestic flight connectivity are driving year-round domestic demand, reducing historical seasonality.

5. **Foreign Direct Investment** — GCC sovereign wealth funds and European hospitality REITs are increasing allocation to Egyptian hospitality assets, attracted by favorable valuations and growth trajectory.

## Competitive Structure

The Egyptian hospitality market features a mix of international brand operators, local chains, and independent hotels:

- **International Brands** — Accor (largest by room count), Marriott, Hilton, IHG, Rotana, Jumeirah, Four Seasons, Ritz-Carlton
- **Local Chains** — TOLIP Hotels, Sunrise Resorts, Jaz Hotels, Pickalbatros, Steigenberger (now HR Group)
- **Ownership** — Predominantly private Egyptian and GCC investors; growing institutional ownership via REITs and hospitality funds

## Regulatory Environment

- **Law 8/1997** — Investment guarantees and incentives for tourism projects
- **Law 119/2008** — Building code compliance including hotel-specific requirements
- **Hotel Classification System** — Ministry of Tourism star rating (1–5 stars) with periodic inspections
- **Environmental Regulations** — Law 4/1994 (Environment Law), increasingly enforced for coastal resorts
- **Tax Incentives** — 50% tax reduction on tourism investment profits (5-year term), customs duty exemptions on imported hotel equipment
