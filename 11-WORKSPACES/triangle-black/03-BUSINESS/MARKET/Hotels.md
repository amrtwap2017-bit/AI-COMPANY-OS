## Ownership Models

### Ownership Structure (Egypt Hotels)

| Ownership Type | Share of Hotels | Characteristics | Engineering Decision-Making |
|---------------|----------------|-----------------|---------------------------|
| Private Individual / Family | 45% | Single hotel or small group; hands-on or absentee owners | Owner or GM decides; cost-sensitive |
| Local Investment Groups | 25% | 3–10 hotels; professional management | Centralized engineering manager, some delegation |
| International REITs / Funds | 15% | Institutional ownership; performance-focused | Corporate engineering standards, delegated operations |
| Government / Public Sector | 8% | State-owned hotels, often legacy properties | Bureaucratic, procurement regulations |
| Brand-Owned | 5% | International chains owning their properties | Strict corporate standards, global procurement |
| Leasehold / Management Contract | 2% | Operator holds long-term lease | Operator responsible for maintenance |

### Operational Models

| Model | Description | % of Hotels | Engineering Implications |
|-------|-------------|------------|-------------------------|
| Management Contract | Brand manages on behalf of owner | 40% | Brand standards apply; owner pays for engineering; procurement may be brand-directed |
| Franchise | Owner operates under brand license | 30% | Owner has more flexibility; brand provides guidelines only |
| Independent | No brand affiliation | 20% | Full owner discretion; price-sensitive |
| Leasehold | Operator leases hotel from owner | 8% | Operator fully responsible for engineering and maintenance |
| Owned & Operated | Owner runs hotel directly | 2% | Direct control over all engineering decisions |

## Hotel Organizational Structure (Engineering)

### Typical Engineering Department Hierarchy

```
General Manager
    └── Director of Engineering / Chief Engineer
            ├── Assistant Chief Engineer
            │       ├── Shift Engineers (x3 rotations)
            │       ├── Maintenance Technicians (HVAC, Electrical, Plumbing, Painting)
            │       └── Pool / Water Treatment Technician
            ├── Preventive Maintenance Coordinator
            ├── Energy Manager (larger hotels only)
            ├── Storekeeper (Spare Parts)
            └── External Contractor Coordinator
```

### Engineering Department Sizing

| Hotel Segment | Engineering Staff (FTE) | In-house vs Outsourced Ratio |
|--------------|------------------------|------------------------------|
| Luxury (400+ rooms) | 15–25 | 70% in-house / 30% outsourced |
| Upscale (250–400 rooms) | 10–18 | 60% in-house / 40% outsourced |
| Midscale (150–250 rooms) | 6–12 | 50% in-house / 50% outsourced |
| Economy (<150 rooms) | 3–6 | 40% in-house / 60% outsourced |

### Key Engineering Roles & Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities | Decision Power | Pain Point |
|------|-----------------|---------------|------------|
| Chief Engineer | Overall engineering operations, budget, compliance, team management | High (within budget) | Overloaded with administrative work, limited time for strategic planning |
| Assistant Chief | Daily operations, shift management, emergency response | Medium | Coordination gaps between shifts |
| Shift Engineer | Shift operations, guest room maintenance, reactive repairs | Low | High turnover, inconsistent skill levels |
| HVAC Technician | Chillers, AHUs, FCUs, refrigeration, AC maintenance | Medium | Most critical skill gap in market |
| Electrical Technician | Switchgear, panels, lighting, generators, BMS | Medium | Safety compliance, aging infrastructure |
| Preventive Maintenance Coordinator | PM schedules, inspections, records | Low | Paper-based tracking, low compliance |
| Storekeeper | Parts inventory, procurement, supplier coordination | Low (buy) | Fragmented supply, stockouts, overstocking |

## Procurement Decision-Making

### Authority Levels

| Procurement Value | Approval Required | Decision Time |
|-----------------|-------------------|---------------|
| Up to EGP 10,000 (~USD 325) | Chief Engineer | Same day |
| EGP 10,000–50,000 (~USD 325–1,625) | General Manager | 1–3 days |
| EGP 50,000–200,000 (~USD 1,625–6,475) | Owner / Corporate Office | 3–14 days |
| Above EGP 200,000 (~USD 6,475) | Board / Investment Committee | 2–4 weeks |

### Brand-Directed vs Open Procurement

| Procurement Type | % of Spend | Description |
|-----------------|-----------|-------------|
| Brand-Mandated (SOE) | 20–35% | Specific equipment brands required by brand standards |
| Preferred Suppliers | 15–25% | Brand-negotiated rates with approved vendors |
| Open / Local Procurement | 40–60% | Left to hotel discretion based on price and availability |

## Implications for Triangle Black

1. **Target segment** — Midscale to Upscale (4–5 star) hotels offer the best balance of engineering complexity, budget availability, and decision-maker accessibility
2. **Decision makers** — Chief Engineer is the primary champion; General Manager is the economic buyer; Owner/Corporate is the strategic approver
3. **Entry strategy** — Start with independent and franchise hotels (owner has more flexibility) before approaching brand-managed properties
4. **Service packaging** — Engineering services must be positioned as budget-compliant, not cost-saving alone (hotels have allocated maintenance budgets)
5. **Brand standards** — Early relationships with Accor, Marriott, and Hilton Egypt engineering teams could unlock multiple properties
6. **Local chain opportunity** — Sunrise, Jaz, and Pickalbatros represent clusters of 8–14 hotels each — high-value accounts with centralized buying

## Business Rules

- Engineering budget estimates based on hotel operating cost benchmarks (25–35% of total OPEX)
- Staff sizing per STR Global hotel operations reports and regional averages
- Procurement authority levels based on Egyptian hospitality industry practices
- Brand vs open procurement split estimated from interviews and industry experience

## KPIs

- Number of hotel accounts by segment (tracking against target mix)
- Average contract value per hotel segment
- Decision-maker contact coverage (% of target accounts)
- Conversion time from first contact to signed agreement

## Risks

- Brand-mandated procurement limits the addressable portion of some hotel budgets
- Owner/operator conflicts can delay engineering decisions
- Chief Engineer turnover is high in Egypt, requiring relationship continuity planning
- Cost-plus vs fixed-price service model acceptance varies by owner type
