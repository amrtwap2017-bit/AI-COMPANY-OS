# Sharm El Sheikh Market Profile

## Overview

Sharm El Sheikh, located at the southern tip of the Sinai Peninsula on the Red Sea, is Egypt's premier resort destination and one of the most established hospitality markets in the Middle East. With approximately **45,000 hotel rooms** across 250+ hotels, the city serves as Triangle Black's initial beachhead market.

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total Hotel Rooms | ~45,000 (e) |
| Number of Hotels | 250+ |
| Annual Visitors (Pre-Pandemic Peak) | ~5 million |
| Average Length of Stay | 7–10 nights |
| Primary Source Markets | Russia, UK, Germany, Italy, Eastern Europe |
| Airport Connectivity | Sharm El Sheikh International Airport (SSH) |
| Airport Capacity | 10 million passengers/year |
| Seasonality | Peak: Oct–Apr; Low: May–Sep (heat) |

## Operational Engineering Landscape

### Current State

- **In-house maintenance teams** dominate — most hotels employ internal engineering departments
- **Outsourced engineering** is limited to specialized MEP contractors for capex projects
- **Preventive maintenance** compliance is inconsistent — many hotels operate reactive models
- **Procurement** is handled locally by hotel purchasing managers with limited supplier management
- **Digital maturity** is low — most hotels use paper-based or basic Excel for maintenance tracking
- **Spare parts inventory** management is fragmented, leading to extended downtime

### Pain Points

| Pain Point | Frequency | Impact |
|-----------|-----------|--------|
| Emergency breakdown response time | High | Revenue loss, guest complaints |
| HVAC failures during peak season | Medium | Significant (comfort-critical for summer) |
| Swimming pool / water treatment issues | Medium | Health compliance, guest satisfaction |
| Electrical system aging | High | Safety risk, operational disruption |
| Spare parts procurement delays | Critical | Extended downtime, high emergency sourcing costs |
| Inefficient preventive maintenance scheduling | High | Accelerated asset depreciation |
| Energy cost management | Medium | 25–35% of hotel operating costs |

## Competitive Dynamics (Sharm El Sheikh)

- **Local MEP contractors** — 15–20 small-to-medium companies serving hotel maintenance and projects
- **International engineering firms** — Present for large capex projects only (e.g., Dar Al-Handasah, Hill International)
- **Hotel supply companies** — ~30 suppliers of MEP equipment, spare parts, and consumables
- **No comprehensive operational engineering partner** exists in the market — this is the gap Triangle Black fills

## Strategic Importance for Triangle Black

1. **Beachhead market** — Highest concentration of hotels within a 30km radius in Egypt
2. **Established tourism infrastructure** — Reliable airport, road network, and business services
3. **Hotel density** — 250+ hotels within reachable service radius reduces dispatch costs
4. **Aging assets** — Many hotels built 1990s–2000s, now requiring significant engineering investment
5. **Owner fragmentation** — Many absentee owners who need reliable operational partners
6. **Pipeline growth** — 12+ new hotels planned, creating new-build opportunities

## Business Rules

- Service radius for Triangle Black operations: 30km from central Sharm (covers all hotel zones)
- Minimum viable client density: 20 hotels within service radius for operational efficiency
- First-year target: 10 hotels under Operational Partnership agreements

- Competition from low-cost local handymen for smaller hotels
