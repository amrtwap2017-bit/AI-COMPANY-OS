# Customer Personas

## Persona 1: Ahmed El-Masry — Hotel Owner / Investor

**Demographics:**
- Age: 45-65
- Nationality: Egyptian or Saudi
- Background: Real estate developer, hospitality investor, family business
- Portfolio: 1-5 hotels (often with partners)
- Education: Business or engineering background

**Typical Day:**
- Spends most time on investment strategy and new developments
- Reviews monthly P&L statements (not operational details)
- Visits properties quarterly or during peak season
- Delegates daily operations to GM

**Pain Points:**
- Engineering opex eating into NOI
- Frequent emergency capex requests from GMs
- Guest complaints about maintenance affecting property reputation
- Inconsistent standards across properties
- Hard to find and retain good chief engineers
- Compliance incidents creating regulatory risk

**Goals:**
- Maximize asset value for eventual sale or refinancing
- Predictable operational costs
- Professional management standards comparable with international chains
- Minimum 20% EBITDA margin from engineering operations

**Objections:**
- "I've tried outsourcing before — it didn't work."
- "My GM handles this."
- "Why should I pay a monthly fee for something I'm already paying for?"
- "You don't understand the unique challenges of my property."

**Sales Approach:**
- Speak the language of ROI, EBITDA, and asset value
- Reference check with other hotel owners
- Offer a no-obligation engineering audit as proof of concept
- Emphasize owner-level reporting and transparency

**Best Channel:** Direct introduction through hospitality association, lawyer, or fellow investor.

## Persona 2: Marco Rossi — General Manager

**Demographics:**
- Age: 38-55
- Nationality: Italian, German, Egyptian, or Lebanese
- Background: F&B or Rooms Division → GM track. 10-20 years in hospitality.
- Hotel: 150-400 rooms, 4-5 star
- Tenure at current property: 2-5 years

**Typical Day:**
- Morning briefing with department heads
- Guest interaction (lobby walks, VIP check-ins)
- Revenue management review
- Operations walk-through
- Evening events or VIP hosting

**Pain Points:**
- Engineering department is a "black box" — no visibility into what they do daily
- Constant complaints from guests about AC, plumbing, room maintenance
- Chief Engineer is hard to manage, has own supplier relationships
- Budget overruns on engineering every month
- Corporate requires compliance certifications but never allocates budget for them
- Spends 3-5 hours/week on engineering issues (should be 1 hour)

**Goals:**
- 90%+ guest satisfaction score for maintenance
- Engineering budget within 2% of forecast
- Compliance audits passed on first attempt
- Focus time on revenue and guest experience, not engineering fires

**Objections:**
- "I don't want to lose my Chief Engineer — he's been here 15 years."
- "This will cause conflict with my existing team."
- "Corporate procurement policies won't allow this."
- "What happens during your Ramadan or holiday staffing gaps?"

**Sales Approach:**
- Emphasize how Triangle Black makes *their* life easier, not less powerful
- Position as a tool for the GM to better manage engineering, not replace it
- Offer a 30-day trial at reduced rate
- Provide reference calls with other GMs

**Best Channel:** LinkedIn outreach, industry events, referral from owner.

## Persona 3: Sayed Hassan — Chief Engineer

**Demographics:**
- Age: 40-60
- Nationality: Egyptian
- Background: Technical institute or engineering degree. Started as technician.
- Experience: 15-30 years in hospitality engineering
- Tenure at current property: 5-10+ years (often lifelong)

**Typical Day:**
- Morning round of plant room checks
- Responding to guest room complaints
- Managing 5-15 technicians
- Dealing with suppliers and contractors
- Paperwork (preventive maintenance logs, purchase requests, compliance docs)

**Pain Points:**
- Chasing suppliers for overdue deliveries
- Repairing equipment with poor-quality spare parts (ordered by purchasing without his input)
- No budget for proper preventive maintenance tools
- Owner/GM don't understand why engineering costs what it does
- Exhausted by peak season breakdowns
- Can't find qualified technicians

**Goals:**
- Run a well-maintained plant that doesn't embarrass him
- Get the supplies and parts he actually needs (not cheapest option)
- Recognition for keeping the hotel running
- Training for his team
- Less firefighting, more planned work

**Objections:**
- "This is a threat to my job."
- "No one knows this hotel's systems better than me."
- "I have my own suppliers I've worked with for 20 years."
- "Another layer of bureaucracy between me and what I need."

**Sales Approach:**
- Respect his experience and knowledge
- Position Triangle Black as his support system, not his replacement
- Show him the supply chain and technical resources he'll gain access to
- Give him a direct line to technical support for his tough problems
- Never undermine his authority in front of his team

**Best Channel:** Internal introduction from GM. Face-to-face meeting.

## Persona 4: Khaled Mansour — Purchasing Manager

**Demographics:**
- Age: 30-50
- Nationality: Egyptian
- Background: Supply chain, business administration, or engineering
- Experience: 5-15 years in hotel procurement
- Reports to: Financial Controller or GM

**Typical Day:**
- Processing purchase requests from all departments
- Sourcing and comparing supplier quotes
- Negotiating with vendors
- Expediting overdue deliveries
- Matching invoices with POs for accounts payable

**Pain Points:**
- 20-30 purchase requests per day across all departments
- Engineering requests are the most complex (technical specs, branded parts)
- Emergency purchases at inflated prices (urgent approval bypass)
- Supplier quality inconsistent — returns and warranty claims are constant
- Storage for engineering spares is chaotic

**Goals:**
- Reduce procurement cycle time
- Lower average cost per purchase order
- Reduce emergency procurement percentage (target: < 10% of total)
- Fewer supplier-related complaints from engineering

**Objections:**
- "This takes away my ability to negotiate better deals."
- "Our ERP system is already set up — this adds complexity."
- "How do I know your prices are actually competitive?"
- "What about my existing supplier relationships?"

**Sales Approach:**
- Show transparent pricing with cost breakdowns
- Demonstrate volume pricing advantage
- Offer to integrate with existing procurement systems
- Respect his role — position Triangle Black as supply chain partner, not replacement

**Best Channel:** Introduction from GM or Financial Controller.

## Persona 5: Youssef Fahmy — Financial Controller

**Demographics:**
- Age: 35-55
- Nationality: Egyptian
- Background: Accounting or finance degree. CPA, CMA, or equivalent.
- Experience: 10-20 years in hospitality finance
- Reports to: GM, with dotted line to ownership

**Typical Day:**
- Reviewing daily revenue and cost reports
- Approving purchase requests above threshold
- Month-end closing and reporting
- Budgeting and forecasting
- Internal audit and compliance

**Pain Points:**
- Engineering budget consistently overrun by 15-25%
- Emergency procurement costs are unpredictable and spike monthly P&L
- Hard to benchmark engineering costs (no industry data)
- Invoices from dozens of vendors — reconciliation is a nightmare
- No visibility into whether engineering spend is preventive or reactive

**Goals:**
- Engineering costs within 2% of budget every month
- Reduction in emergency spend year-over-year
- Clean audit trail for all engineering procurement
- Benchmarkable KPIs for engineering operations
- Single invoice from Triangle Black instead of 20+ vendor invoices

**Objections:**
- "Monthly retainer adds fixed cost when we need variable flexibility."
- "How do I verify you're passing on the best supplier prices?"
- "Currency risk — if you import, how is pricing protected?"
- "This needs board approval — takes 2-3 months."

**Sales Approach:**
- Lead with cost predictability argument
- Show comparison: total cost of current vendor chaos vs. Triangle Black
- Demonstrate transparent pricing model
- Offer pilot with minimal financial commitment
- Provide written commercial terms with FX clause

**Best Channel:** GM introduces. Or direct approach with financial benchmarking data.
