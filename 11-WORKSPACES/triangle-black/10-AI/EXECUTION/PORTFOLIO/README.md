# Portfolio Management

## Overview

The Portfolio Management function provides the strategic framework for organizing, prioritizing, and governing programs under enterprise strategic objectives. It ensures that investments across the organization align with business goals, deliver measurable value, and operate within defined risk and resource constraints.

A portfolio is the highest-level organizational construct in the enterprise execution model. It groups related programs, projects, and operational work under shared strategic themes, enabling centralized oversight, trade-off decisions, and value optimization.

## How Portfolios Organize Programs Under Strategic Objectives

Portfolios provide the bridge between enterprise strategy and program execution. Each portfolio is mapped to one or more strategic objectives, and programs within the portfolio contribute directly to achieving those objectives. The structure follows:

- **Strategic Objectives** define what the enterprise seeks to achieve (growth, efficiency, compliance, innovation).
- **Portfolios** group programs aligned to those objectives and serve as the vehicle for investment governance.
- **Programs** execute the work required to deliver outcomes that realize the objectives.
- **Projects and Epics** are the tactical units within programs that produce tangible deliverables.

This hierarchy ensures traceability from the boardroom to the delivery team, with each level accountable to the one above.

## Portfolio Governance

Portfolio governance establishes the decision rights, review cadence, and escalation paths that ensure disciplined investment management. Key governance components include:

- **Portfolio Review Board (PRB):** Senior leadership body that approves new initiatives, reviews portfolio performance, and reallocates investment across programs.
- **Investment Review Committee (IRC):** Cross-functional group responsible for assessing business cases, scoring proposals, and recommending funding decisions.
- **Portfolio Management Office (PMO):** The operational arm that maintains portfolio data, facilitates reviews, tracks KPIs, and produces reporting.
- **Escalation Path:** Issues that cannot be resolved at the program level are escalated to the PRB for portfolio-level trade-off decisions.

Governance cadences include quarterly strategic reviews, monthly investment reviews, and weekly portfolio health checks.

## Portfolio Lifecycle

The portfolio lifecycle defines the stages through which initiatives pass from conception to completion:

1. **Define** — Strategic objectives are established or refreshed. Portfolio scope is defined. Market conditions and enterprise capabilities are assessed.
2. **Prioritize** — Initiatives are proposed, scored against strategic fit and value, and ranked. Investment allocations are determined.
3. **Approve** — Selected initiatives receive formal funding authorization. Program charters are approved. Resources are allocated.
4. **Execute** — Programs deliver against their charters. Portfolio oversight monitors progress, risk, and value realization.
5. **Review** — Portfolio performance is assessed against objectives. Lessons learned are captured. The portfolio is rebalanced for the next cycle.

## Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|------|----------------|
| Portfolio Director | Owns portfolio strategy, chairs PRB, accountable for portfolio outcomes |
| Portfolio Manager | Manages day-to-day portfolio operations, maintains KPIs, coordinates reviews |
| Program Manager | Leads individual programs, reports to portfolio, manages program execution |
| Investment Analyst | Evaluates business cases, tracks investment performance, calculates ROI |
| Governance Lead | Manages review cadence, maintains decision log, ensures compliance |
| Value Architect | Defines value models, tracks value realization, advises on value optimization |

## Integration with Enterprise Blueprint

Portfolios consume capabilities from the Enterprise Blueprint (Program 1) to inform strategic planning and execution. Blueprint capabilities — including reference architecture patterns, data standards, security policies, and platform services — are treated as enterprise assets that portfolios leverage to accelerate delivery, reduce risk, and ensure consistency.

Portfolio feedback loops inform blueprint evolution: gaps identified during program execution are fed back to the blueprint team for continuous improvement.
