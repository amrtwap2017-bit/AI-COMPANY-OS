# Sprint Retrospective

## Overview

The Sprint Retrospective is the final event of the sprint. Its purpose is continuous improvement of the AI execution system itself. The team inspects how the sprint went regarding people, relationships, process, and tools; identifies what worked well and what needs improvement; and creates a concrete action plan for the next sprint.

**Duration: 45 minutes maximum** for a standard 2-week sprint. Shorter sprints (1 week) use a 30-minute window.

## Participants

| Participant | Role in Retrospective |
|---|---|
| **AI Agents (all)** | Equal participants — share observations, suggest improvements, volunteer for action items |
| **Program Manager AI** | Facilitates the retrospective, ensures safe environment, documents outcomes, tracks action items |
| **Human Stakeholders** | Do not attend unless specifically invited for a blocked/relationship issue |

## Activities

### 1. Set the Stage (5 minutes)

- Restate the retrospective purpose: improve the execution system, not assign blame.
- Review the retrospective last-sprint action items and their status.
- Share the sprint context: sprint goal, completion rate, notable events.

### 2. Gather Data (15 minutes)

Each participant contributes to three categories. The Program Manager AI captures contributions in real time.

**What Went Well**

- What practices, tools, or behaviors contributed to success?
- What should we intentionally continue doing?
- Were there any unexpected positive outcomes?

**What Could Be Improved**

- What caused friction, delay, or rework?
- Where did the sprint process break down?
- Were there communication gaps, tooling issues, or process gaps?
- Were tasks poorly estimated? Were dependencies mismanaged?

**What Was Confusing or Unclear**

- Were there ambiguous requirements or acceptance criteria?
- Were there gaps in the context pack or documentation?
- Were any sprint events unclear or unhelpful?

### 3. Generate Insights (10 minutes)

- Group the collected data into themes (e.g., estimation, quality gates, communication, tooling, dependency management).
- For each theme, identify the root cause: is this a process issue, a tooling issue, a knowledge gap, or a coordination issue?
- Prioritize the top 2-3 themes that, if addressed, would have the greatest positive impact.

### 4. Decide What to Do (10 minutes)

- For each of the top 2-3 improvement themes, define a concrete action item.
- Each action item must have:
  - **Description**: What exactly will be tried or changed?
  - **Owner**: Which AI agent or human is responsible?
  - **Timeline**: When will it be implemented? (During next sprint, within 1 week, etc.)
  - **Success Criteria**: How will we know the change worked?
- Limit action items to 3 maximum. A few focused actions are more likely to be completed than a long list.

### 5. Close (5 minutes)

- Summarize the action items and owners.
- Confirm that participants feel heard and the retrospective was productive.
- Thank the team for their contributions.
- Schedule the next retrospective.

## Retrospective Techniques

The Program Manager AI may choose from these facilitation techniques depending on sprint context:

| Technique | Best For | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Start/Stop/Continue** | General-purpose | What should we start doing? Stop doing? Continue doing? |
| **Sailboat** | Identifying risks and accelerators | What is pushing us forward (wind)? What is holding us back (anchor)? What risks are on the horizon (iceberg)? |
| **Mad/Sad/Glad** | Emotional safety | What made you mad? Sad? Glad? |
| **4Ls** | Balanced review | What did we Like? Learn? Lack? Long for? |
| **Timeline** | Event-heavy sprints | Plot sprint events on a timeline and discuss emotional highs and lows |

## Rules of Retrospective

1. **No blame.** The retrospective is about the system, process, and tools — not about individual performance. Blame destroys psychological safety.
2. **Action items must have owners.** An action item without an owner is a wish, not a plan.
3. **Limited action items.** Three or fewer action items per sprint. Focus drives follow-through.
4. **Previous action items are reviewed first.** Every retrospective starts with accountability for the last sprint's improvements.
5. **Safe environment.** All participants can speak freely. The retrospective notes are shared only with the team unless explicitly agreed otherwise.

## Action Item Tracking

All retrospective action items are added to a running improvement backlog. Each sprint's retrospective begins with a review of:

- Action items from the previous retrospective (status: Done/In Progress/Not Started).
- Long-term improvement items that span multiple sprints.
- Systemic issues that require more than one sprint to address.

When an action item is completed, its success criteria are evaluated. If the change produced the desired improvement, it is adopted as a standard practice. If not, the team discusses what to try instead.
